5 Product-Led Growth Loops Inside GetMusic

No special intro needed. Let the title land. The subtitle does the setup work.

Slide 02

What GetMusic Does

Quick context. GetMusic is a Bandcamp code distribution and discovery platform for independent music.

Bandcamp artists have download codes — basically gift cards for digital albums — that they want to get into the right hands.

Fans want discovery, not catalogs. They want someone to surface the right stuff.

GetMusic sits in the middle: it distributes codes, organizes releases, promotes artists, and keeps recirculating the catalog so nothing gets buried.

One important thing: I'm solo. No team, no employees. Everything I'm about to describe is run by me plus some Rails jobs.

Slide 03

Opening Provocation: Most Side Projects Bolt Marketing On Afterward

Most side projects have a product phase, then a separate "marketing phase." They ship, then they hustle.

I think that split is a trap. If your product doesn't create its own distribution, you are permanently in hustle mode. And if you're a solo founder, that means you lose.

The question I asked: What if normal product usage created the raw material for growth? Not a separate campaign. A system.

Slide 04

The Dangerous Failure Is Silence

Okay, before I show you how distribution is built in, I want to show you why I had to build it that way.

GetMusic automates a lot: nightly Bandcamp scraping, payment reconciliation, weekly playlist assembly, winback emails. But the scariest failure is not a crash. A crash screams. You see it.

The scariest failure is silence. A job that just stops running. You don't find out until weeks later, when an artist asks why their release never went out.

So I built a Scheduled Checks system. It watches every background job and shows me red, yellow, or green on a dashboard.

The design decision that matters most: Health is never stored. It's computed live, every few minutes, from now versus the last successful run.

If a job stops checking in, the dashboard turns red immediately. A dead thing can't raise its hand to say it died. So the system doesn't wait for it.

Slide 05

Product → Automation → AI Ops

Three layers. Only one is AI.

  1. Product layer. The product creates distribution opportunities: artist uploads, code drops, profiles, featured surfaces, Spotlights, IndieCrates, radio matches. I didn't invent these with AI. I designed the product so these moments exist.
  2. Automation layer. Background jobs, queues, scheduled posts, webhooks, notification triggers. Boring, but critical. Without this, the AI outputs would just become more chores.
  3. AI layer. The product creates structured inputs. AI turns them into language: summaries, social copy, tag descriptions, playlist blurbs, featured posts, moderation checks.
AI is not the growth strategy. AI makes the growth strategy operable by one person.
Slide 06

Loop 1: Uploads Become Distribution Moments

When an artist uploads a release on GetMusic, that upload is not the end of the workflow. It's the start.

The release gets a page. Code distribution is set up. A summary is generated. Social posts are queued across six platforms. The release enters the catalog for future recirculation.

All of that is triggered from a single upload.

Design product actions so they create reusable assets. The upload is not the end — it is the start of the distribution loop.
Slide 07

Loop 2: Artist Metadata Becomes Attribution and Reach

When artists sign up, they add names, links, social handles, genres, locations. Most products store that in a database and forget about it.

On GetMusic, that metadata becomes distribution infrastructure.

When we post about an artist, we tag them. That tag gives them a reason to engage — like, reply, reshare. Every post becomes a notification. And every notification is a potential amplification.

Every piece of user-provided metadata can become future distribution context.
Slide 08

Loop 3: Notifications Create Reshare Moments

Artists are notified when GetMusic features them: new release, popular pick, radio play, Spotlight feature.

A notification is not just an operational update. It's a distribution trigger. It tells the artist at the exact moment they have something to be proud of.

They reshare. More fans see the post. More fans visit GetMusic. The catalog gets another hit.

If the product creates a moment someone is proud of, tell them at the exact moment they are most likely to share it.
Slide 09

Loop 4: Spotlights Turn Artists Into Co-Marketers

Artists fill out a form: bio, origin story, creative process, latest release inspiration, fan engagement.

Then GetMusic turns those raw inputs into a polished feature story.

Because the artist wrote the raw material, they have emotional ownership. That ownership makes them more likely to share it.

Both sides distribute: GetMusic publishes the story, the artist shares it to their audience. It's co-marketing without a co-marketing meeting.

The artist is not just the subject of the content. They help create the raw material, which makes them more likely to distribute it.
Slide 10

Loop 5: Distribution Satellites Extend the Surface Area

GetMusic is not just one release page. It's surrounded by satellites:

  • IndieCrates — a weekly curated crate, weighted toward active artists
  • Radio Play — matches DJ tracklists to GetMusic catalog, creating credibility backlinks
  • Featured Posts, Popular Releases, Playlists, SonicSift

Each surface gives a release another chance to be discovered, often months after the upload moment has passed.

A release I onboarded two years ago can still be posted today, in copy I never wrote.
Slide 11

Where AI Actually Fits

This is the AI Side-Project Showcase, so let's be honest about where AI shows up.

I don't have an editor, a social manager, a moderator, a curator, and a copywriter. I have product events, Rails jobs, prompts, and review points.

Walk through the table briefly:

  • Release summaries: editorial intern
  • Social copy: six platform managers
  • Tag descriptions: genre copywriter
  • Playlist blurbs: newsletter writer
  • Featured posts: blogger
  • Offensive material: trust and safety reviewer

All of it runs on Claude with prompts stored in the database. I tune copy without deploying code.

Slide 12

The Moat Is the Loop, Not the LLM

Here's the honest truth: AI can help anyone build features faster. So code alone is not defensible.

What is defensible is the loop that turns every product action into a distribution moment.

Releases, artists, spotlights, tags, playlists, radio matches, social posts — every one of them is a distribution node. The LLM helps operate the machine. The machine only exists because the product was designed to produce it.

Don't ask, "Where can I add AI?" Ask, "What repeated product events create useful raw material?" Then use AI to turn that raw material into distribution.
